# AI Transparency & Responsible Use Notice

**In short:** AmazAI is an AI agent service. Its companions can generate content and, when authorized, use tools and connected accounts to take actions. AI results can be wrong. Users must review consequential work and keep appropriate human control.

## 1. What the Service does

AmazAI allows people and organizations to configure AI companions, rooms, routines, files, approvals, and connectors. A companion may research information, generate or edit content, operate a browser or computer, call tools, communicate through a connected service, or prepare an action for approval.

The Service uses probabilistic models. The same instruction may produce different results, and an output may be inaccurate, incomplete, outdated, biased, infringing, or unsuitable for its intended context.

## 2. Models and providers

AmazAI currently provides model inference through Amazon Bedrock. The underlying model may vary by feature, availability, customer configuration, and service tier. Changing a model can change output style, performance, limitations, and error patterns.

Relevant prompts, instructions, conversation context, files, and connector results may be sent to the configured model service to generate a response or proposed action. Current operational providers are described in our [Subprocessor List](/subprocessors).

AmazAI does not use Customer Content to train or fine-tune a general-purpose model and does not authorize its production model service to do so. That commitment does not mean content is never processed or retained: content must be processed to produce results and may be retained for limited operational or security purposes under the applicable configuration and agreement.

## 3. Human control and approvals

Users control which accounts are connected, which permissions are granted, which routines are enabled, and which actions require approval. AmazAI may require an additional approval for a high-impact or irreversible action. Approval gates reduce risk but do not guarantee that an approved action is correct, lawful, or reversible.

Approving a proposed action—or configuring a routine to run within granted permissions without a further prompt—authorizes AmazAI and its providers to attempt that action. Users should grant the narrowest permissions needed, inspect material inputs and recipients, set spend and action limits where available, and disable access that is no longer required.

## 4. Consequential decisions

AmazAI is not a substitute for professional judgment and does not provide legal, medical, financial, employment, tax, or other professional advice. Do not rely on an AI output as the sole basis for a decision about a person’s employment, housing, credit, insurance, education, healthcare, legal rights, benefits, safety, or access to essential services.

Some high-risk uses are prohibited or require a separately reviewed enterprise deployment even when a person is present. See the [Acceptable Use Policy](/acceptable-use).

## 5. Communications and generated content

People should know when they are interacting with an AI system or receiving materially AI-generated content when disclosure is required by law or needed to avoid deception. A customer that directs an agent to communicate with a third party is responsible for the content, recipient, authority, and legally required disclosure.

Users must not remove provenance information, misrepresent an AI system as a person, or use synthetic content to deceive or materially harm another person. Where a feature produces content that must carry a machine-readable mark or human-readable label, users must preserve that mark or label when publishing or distributing the content.

## 6. Testing, feedback, and incidents

AmazAI evaluates the Service and its safeguards over time, but no test covers every prompt, tool, account, or real-world condition. Report harmful output, unexpected actions, or suspected misuse to **security@amazai.co**. Security vulnerabilities should be reported under our [Security & Responsible Disclosure](/security-disclosure) guidelines.
